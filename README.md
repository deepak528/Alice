# Alice in Mazeland

A local-only maze game. Double-click `index.html` to play in Edge or Chrome — no internet, no install. Keep the `vendor` folder next to it: it holds Three.js, which draws the 3D view.

Guide Alice through a hedge maze to **Home** 🏡 or **Wonderland** ✨. Creatures block the way; find the right tool hidden in the maze and walk into the creature to send it off.

**The Story** 📖 plays its parts in order, always from the beginning:

1. **Follow the White Rabbit** 🐰 is an intro, not a puzzle, and it can't be lost.
   - **The garden:** the left of the map is the lawn at home. Alice's house stands at the top, and her sister sits reading on a picnic blanket next to Dinah the kitten, who is asleep.
   - **The opening (about 18 s):** the storyteller reads the captions aloud, and her sister asks "What is the use of a book without pictures?" Then the White Rabbit hops up the lawn: "Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late! Follow me, Alice!", and the storyteller gives the goal. Only one caption or speech bubble shows at a time. A tap, Space or Enter skips to his "Follow me"; Alice can walk once he has said it.
   - **The sky:** soft clouds, and every so often a striped hot-air balloon drifts across, towing a banner with "Samyra" written in cloud.
   - **The maze:** a small, low hedge maze on the right, with no fog and no mini-map, so the Rabbit always shows over the hedges and is the one to follow.
   - **The Rabbit:** he runs to each corner and waits there, tapping his watch, until Alice is close. After the opening he says nothing more; paw prints show the way he went, fading after a while. On the way he drops his white kid gloves and his fan. Picking them up is optional, and he thanks her for them.
   - **Taps:** in the garden, tapping the sister, Dinah, the flowers, the house, the butterflies or the Rabbit makes them answer.
   - **The end:** he dives into a rabbit hole under a big old oak and drops a little DRINK ME bottle. Alice is too big for the hole until she drinks it and shrinks; then the hole pulls her in by itself, step by step. As she steps in, the stopwatch sticker ⏱️ goes in her sticker book, with a banner, confetti and the storyteller saying "A stopwatch sticker for your book!"
   - **Map size:** the map is 25×17 on Easy, 27×17 on Medium and 29×19 on Hard. There are no creatures, tools or riddles.
2. **The fall**: a slow, dreamy nine-second fall down the rabbit hole, past cupboards, bookshelves, maps, jars and clocks, with the rush of air and Alice's soft "Aaaah…". It always plays in full (no skipping). In the 2D view it's a "Down, down, down…" card. The White Rabbit loses his watch on the way down.
3. **Tea Party** ☕: the Queen's rose garden, with dark clipped hedges dotted with red and white roses, rosy gravel and golden evening light.
   - **The landing lawn:** Alice floats gently down onto an open lawn on the left and lands on her feet, full size (the DRINK ME wore off in the fall). Her knees give a little, she sways a moment, dizzy, seeing stars, then stands up straight; she never tips over, and the camera stays level with her. The White Rabbit is there to welcome her, in his own voice: "Hello again, Alice, and welcome to Wonderland! It's a curious place, full of curious things. I must hurry along now… but look out for me, we'll meet again!" Then he hops off, leaving Alice to find out about the little doors for herself. A tap, Space or Enter skips the welcome.
   - **The lawn's garden:** a stone fountain with sparkling drops near its bottom and a wooden bench along its top edge (Alice walks round both; ⛲ and 🪑 on the flat map), flower beds along its edges and the door hedge, and four butterflies flitting over it.
   - **Three little doors** stand side by side in the lawn's hedge, painted pink, blue and yellow, each with a soft glow that breathes in and out so they catch the eye from across the lawn. Full-size Alice can't fit through any of them; the first time she tries, the door says gently "Alice, you are too big to fit through here." A red-spotted **mushroom** is tucked into a corner of the lawn behind a bush: one nibble and she shrinks (30% of her height), too small now for anyone to hear her, so friends just block her way. In 3D the camera comes down with her while she's tiny, so the hedges tower overhead.
   - Behind two of the doors a short way turns a corner, up, and ends at a **DEAD END** sign (it only shows once she's round the corner). The third, a different one each game, goes on into the maze.
   - **The Caterpillar** hides on his red-spotted mushroom at the end of a side path off the way on, a few turns past the doors, so Alice has to explore to find him. Walking into him, smoke rings float up and a card asks "Who… are… YOU?" with three pictures: Alice, the White Rabbit and the Queen. Tapping anyone but Alice gets a puff of smoke and "I think not!" (try again). Tapping Alice earns a bite of his mushroom: she grows back to her proper size and he waves goodbye.
   - **The latch gate:** on the way on, just past where the Caterpillar's side path leaves it, a white garden gate crosses the way. Its gold latch is far too high for tiny Alice, so she has to grow first; then she lifts the latch and it swings open.
   - **The singing flowers** (optional): in one more dead end, a rose, a daisy and a tulip as tall as Alice's waist, with little faces, sway and turn to watch her. Walking into them, they ask aloud "Would you like to sing with us, Alice?", then sing a three-note tune, each flower lighting up as it sings. "Now you: tap the flowers in the same order!" A wrong flower and they just sing it again; "Play the tune again" replays it and "Done for now" leaves it for later. Got it right: "Beautiful singing, Alice!", and they sing her on her way.
   - **Paint the roses red** (optional): three white rose bushes stand in dead ends. Walking into one opens a card with its five white roses; each tap paints one red. The top bar counts the bushes painted (🌹 0/3). All three red earns a gold star ⭐ from the Queen, shown at the end. "Done for now" leaves the rest for later.
   - **The Cheshire Cat's grin:** when Alice seems lost (standing still for about 8 seconds, or three dead ends in a row), a floating grin fades in at the next junction on her way and points which way to go: to the mushroom on the lawn, to the Caterpillar while she's tiny, then to her chair at the tea party, then to the garden gate. The first time, he says "This way, my dear!" aloud; after that something like "We're all mad here!" in his bubble. Then he fades away.

The Tea Party maze is the same size as Chapter 1's (23×13 on Easy, the only level the menu offers now), with the landing lawn (seven tiles across) and its little doors on its left. At its far corner it opens onto a lawn six tiles wide, on the right, where the tea party is laid.

There are no tools in the Tea Party and nobody can hurt Alice. Two friends wait in the maze, drawn from the Mad Hatter, the Cheshire Cat, the Dog and the White Rabbit, and the Mad Hatter is always one of them (he hurries back to his seat at the table after). Walking into a friend brings up a riddle on their theme (the Hatter's are about hats, tea and cake, the White Rabbit's about time, the Cat's about grins and vanishing, the Dog's about bones and play), with three picture answers. The friend reads the riddle and its three answers aloud in their own voice ("…Is it a balloon, your age, or rain?"), and their reply too, so children who can't read yet can answer on their own. The riddle card sits on the right of the screen, over the maze left undimmed, so Alice and the friend asking stay in view; the mini-map and the on-screen arrows step aside until it's answered (the Caterpillar's question and the tea party's puzzle cards work the same way). A wrong answer just brings another riddle, as many times as it takes; Alice is never sent back. A right answer makes the friend step aside.

**The tea party** ☕ is laid on the lawn past the maze. A ☕ marker shows it on the map and mini-map, even through the fog. Once Alice is out on the lawn the maze is done, so the mini-map goes away. Through the whole scene there's one thing to read at a time: each caption has gone before the next speech bubble, each bubble before the next line, and nothing is said in both.
- **The table:** a long table, twice the old size, set low on the lawn near the way in, with six cups and a cake stand. Above it, the croquet ground (soldier hoops and hedgehogs) is always set up and in view, so Alice sees it before the game starts.
- **The guests:** the Mad Hatter and the Queen of Hearts stand at the table's two ends, the March Hare along its far side. The Dormouse is asleep in a teapot on it. There's one empty chair, at the near end.
- **Arriving:** as Alice walks out onto the lawn, the guests cry "No room! No room!", then the Queen gives in: "…Oh, all right. Take a seat, Alice!" Walking into a guest gets a line from them.
- **Sitting down:** when Alice walks into the chair, the arrows stop working, the chair slides out and she sits. In 3D the camera rises behind her to watch the table. The March Hare says "Have some tea! Will you pour, Alice?"
- **Pour the tea:** a card shows the three guests, each with an empty cup under them (the next one ringed) and a dashed gold line in it, and a big teapot. Hold the teapot to pour (it tips, and the tea rises), and let go once the tea is up to the gold line: each guest says thank you in their own voice ("Thank you kindly!", "Ooh, lovely!", "Delightful!"). Let go too soon and it's "A little more!"; pour on to the brim and the cup wobbles and spills ("Oops! It spilt over. Try again!") and is emptied. Every line is read aloud. With all three poured, everyone sips.
- **The raven:** the Hatter asks "Why is a raven like a writing-desk?", Alice gives up, and he hasn't the slightest idea either. This part is just for fun.
- **Decorate the cake:** "It's an unbirthday party! Let's decorate the cake, Alice!" A card shows the cake, the toppings the Hatter asks for as a row of pictures (the next one ringed), and five big topping buttons: a cherry, a candle, a star, a strawberry and a sweet. He asks for two on Easy, three on Medium and four on Hard. Tap them in his order: each right one goes on the cake; any other gets a gentle "Not that one!", with as many tries as needed.
- **The Hatter's riddle:** "Let's see if you can solve MY riddle!" A riddle pops up with three picture answers. A wrong answer brings another riddle, as many times as it takes, and never sends Alice away.
- **The golden key** 🔑: the right answer earns it from the Queen. Alice holds it up. The key shows in her hand and in the top bar.
- **Croquet with the Queen** 🦔: then the Queen asks, "Do you play croquet? Then let's play!" They play it out in 3D on the ground above the table, with live hedgehogs for balls and three playing-card soldiers bent over for hoops (the next one ringed in gold). Alice and the Queen each walk over to their own hedgehog (the other waits at the side), and the camera sits behind the hedgehog in play, following it as it rolls. The card on the right has just two arrows and a big **Swing** button: the arrows turn Alice's aim (a dotted line on the grass, already pointing at her next soldier), and holding Swing makes a power bar rise and fall; letting go hits (the dots light up gold as the power rises). On a computer, Left and Right aim and Space swings. Without 3D, the card shows the lawn from above instead. Through all three soldiers in order wins. Going through a soldier earns another go; otherwise it's the Queen's turn. She takes her own shots and says how they went, but only aims true while she's behind, so Alice always wins. Then the Hatter has an unbirthday present for her.
- **The unbirthday present** 🎁: "And here is an unbirthday present for you, Alice!" A wrapped present wiggles on a card; tap it and it opens to a teapot sticker 🫖 for her sticker book ("A teapot sticker for your sticker book!", with confetti). Then Alice gets up from the table and can walk again.
- **Captions:** storybook captions tell each beat, and everything at the table is spoken: the storyteller reads the captions and each guest speaks in their own voice. On the cake card the Hatter reads out his order and answers each tap, and at the riddle he reads out his question and the reply. A tap, Space or Enter skips to the next beat (not while a puzzle or the riddle is up). The puzzle cards have big buttons for a finger on an iPad, and work with a mouse, or Tab and Enter, too.
- **The exit** is a garden gate with a golden padlock, in the hedge at the lawn's far side. It stays locked until Alice has the key, then swings open, and a "Through the garden gate…" card (a tap or any key skips it) leads on into the Tulgey Wood.

4. **The Tulgey Wood** 🌲: a sunny wood on a bright summer morning: leafy trees above the undergrowth, toadstools, fireflies and a blue sky. Alice is back at her proper size, and she's looking for the **Cheshire Cat**. It goes in order, gentle to exciting: a soft start in a little meadow, the bridge, a little bush maze, the soldiers, then a maze the size of Chapter 1's (23×13) with the Jabberwock as the finale, and the Cat's party at the end. There's no shrinking or growing here and no illusion hedges: no DRINK ME or EAT ME, roots, thorns, ghost or fake paths.
   - **The soft start:** Alice starts in a little meadow, safe behind a short bush, several steps from the bridge. The White Rabbit waits a little ahead of her, to her left. As in Chapter 1's opening, one line at a time: the chapter's title and a caption, then the Rabbit slowly explains it all (aloud): "Psst! Alice! Over here!", "The Cheshire Cat is hiding somewhere in this wood. Can you find him?", "First, cross the bridge over the river. A plank is missing… but the fish will help you.", "Then sneak past the Queen's card soldiers. They're hunting for the Cat too!", "Hide in the bushes, and only creep out when they look the other way.", "And beware the Jabberwock! If it comes, run to the Cat." and "The fireflies will show you the way. Follow me!" Then he hops off across the meadow, up the bridge, over the gap in one big hop, and waits on the far side, facing her. A tap, Space or Enter skips the talk (he still says a short version). Played again, he just says "Back again? Over the bridge, past the soldiers, and find the Cat!"
   - **The White Rabbit** gives every tip: from the far side of the gap he explains the fish; then he meets her again at the end of the bush maze (see below).
   - **The bridge:** a river 9 tiles wide runs across the whole wood, and the only way over is a long, tall, red-lacquered arched bridge with gold-capped posts. The camera rises with Alice as she walks up it. Five koi swim slowly up and down the river below, and now and then one makes a tiny hop out of the water. Under the bridge's middle swim three **glowing fish** (violet, gold and blue, bigger, each with a soft glow on the water round it) and a big **greedy grey fish**, each to and fro at its own pace. A plank is missing from the very top, broken into three pieces: Alice can't cross the gap. At its edge she sits down, legs over the gap, and leans over to watch the water (the camera swings round behind her, looking down into the river). The Rabbit, across the gap, says (aloud): "A plank is missing! Sit down at the edge, Alice, and look into the water…", "The plank broke into three pieces! Feed each glowing fish just as it swims under the gap, and it brings up a piece." and "But don't feed the greedy grey fish, or a koi. A piece will slip back!" On the **Feed the glowing fish** card (it shows how many pieces are back, "1 of 3"), each tap of the bread drops crumbs through the gap. The timing is tight: they must land while a glowing fish that hasn't helped yet is right under the gap (within 0.6 of a tile). Then he leaps for them, dives, and pushes his piece back up. If the greedy fish is under the gap he gobbles them; otherwise a koi snaps them up. Either way, the last piece put back slips into the water again, and the fish who brought it must be fed again. With all three pieces in, the Rabbit says "Well done, Alice! Now through the bushes… quietly. The soldiers are near!" and hops off, and the koi leap one after another beside the bridge: "Thank you, Alice! The Cheshire Cat went that way, past the soldiers!" ("Done for now" closes the card; walking at the gap again opens it.)
   - **The bush maze:** past the bridge, a little maze of tall bushes (7 tiles across, taller than the bridge's top) hides the soldiers' clearing, so Alice can't see them from the bridge. There's never a straight way through it. At its end the White Rabbit is waiting in the short bush right before the soldiers. One line at a time (aloud; a tap skips it): "Psst! Alice! In here, in the bush!", "Just past this bush, the Queen's card soldiers march up and down. You must get across without being seen!", "Each soldier sees the yellow patch in front of him. Hide in the bushes, and only creep out when he looks away.", "But mind the dry brown bushes: they rustle, and a soldier will turn to look! And one soldier sometimes turns round." and "If they catch you, they'll chain you up for a while!" Then he looks at his watch: "Oh my ears and whiskers, look at the time! I'm late, I'm late!" and vanishes.
   - **Sneak past the soldiers:** past the bush maze, a sunny clearing 16 tiles across where four of the Queen's card soldiers, hunting for the Cat, hold spears. Each one marches up and down his own column, one step every 0.8 s, turning round at the top and bottom (they take turns: one starts at the top, the next at the bottom). In front of each, a soft yellow V on the grass shows what he can see: up to 3 tiles ahead, a tile wider each side for every tile ahead. Thirteen leafy bushes stand in five columns; in each column of three, one is a **dry, rustly bush** (autumn-brown, 🍂 on the map). When Alice steps into a dry bush, the nearest soldier (within 4 columns) turns to look her way and stands still for 4 beats with a "!" over his head ("Crackle, crackle!"), and while he looks, the dry bush doesn't hide her. The third soldier **turns round** now and then (every 15 beats), stands looking the other way for 5 beats with a "!", then turns back and marches on. In a bush Alice is hidden: she ducks down inside it so only her bow peeks out, and the bush glows softly green and rustles. She's hidden even right under a soldier's nose. Behind the short bush at the clearing's edge she's safe too (it glows the same way). Out in his V, or walking into him, a horn blows (ta-ta-taaa!), his V flashes red, a "!" pops up over his head, and Alice freezes where she stands. He cries "Halt! Have you seen the Cheshire Cat? Into chains with you!" (aloud), and he and the nearest other soldier walk up to stand either side of her, just behind. The soldiers put an iron chain round Alice with a big brass padlock on her back, lower their spears to point at her, and march her all the way back into the end of the bush maze; then they go back to their posts. She can't take a step (the chain just rattles) until the padlock breaks loose 20 seconds later; the top line counts the seconds down, and the padlock shakes harder at the end. Then "Clink!", the chain falls off, and she's free to try again. After the third catch, the soldiers get tired and march a beat slower ("The soldiers are getting tired…"), so no child gets stuck. The tests check that all 200 test woods can be sneaked through. There are no fireflies until she's past, since the soldiers would see them. Past the last soldier, the Cat's grin appears: "Psst! This way, Alice, before they find me!"
   - **The Cat's riddle:** halfway, his grin hangs in front of a wall of trees and asks a riddle on the right-side card, reading it and its answers aloud (like the Tea Party's riddles). As many tries as she needs (a wrong answer brings another). The right answer melts the trees away, showing a hidden path.
   - **Wood creatures:** a pencil-bird, a mirror-bird, an umbrella-bird and a horn-duck waddle about in dead ends off the way. Bumping one gets a scribble, a glint, a "Plip!" or a "HONK!"; they never block the way.
   - **The grin** still shows the way when Alice seems lost: to the riddle, then to the Cat, then to the portal.
   - **The fireflies:** a few brighter, golden fireflies always hover over the next 4 tiles along the right way (never past the Cat's wall of trees before the riddle opens it).
   - **The Jabberwock** 🐉 (the finale): three-quarters of the way along, past the riddle, it bursts out of the trees 4 tiles behind her ("Run to the Cheshire Cat!") and chases her, one tile every 0.4 s, a little slower than she walks. If it catches her, she tumbles 5 tiles back along the way (from where she left it, if she'd strayed) and it flaps off for good. If she finds the Cat first, it sees his grin and flaps away. It chases only once.
   - **Sounds:** now and then (every 10 to 20 seconds) an owl hoots or something rustles in the leaves, and the Jabberwock roars.
   - **The end:** the Cat sits on a low branch of a big twisted tree, one step before the exit. Walking into him: "You found me! We're all mad here, you know." He fades away, grin last, and the portal glows open. He leaves the 😸 sticker for the sticker book, with confetti. The first time, the Cat's party follows: his grin says "A party, for finding me!" and more confetti falls. Then the chapter's card: **Through the portal ▶** (a "Through the looking-glass…" card leads on to the chessboard) or **Play again** (the same wood, with the short opening). Played again, the party is quick, so the card comes up after a moment. **R** restarts only the wood.

5. **The Looking-Glass** ♟️: a chessboard of ivory-white and soft-black squares, 8 by 8, each square 2 steps across (16 steps a side), on a lawn inside a low hedge, under a bright afternoon sky. The board covers about 80% of the lawn: a strip of lawn a step wide runs down each side (only to look at; Alice can't walk on it), and a strip two steps deep lies past the far edge. Dinah's kitten followed Alice through the looking-glass and waits out on that far strip 🐱. Finding her leads them through a hedge maze to the White Queen's castle, and home: this is the last chapter.
   - **The opening:** the storyteller sets the scene, then the White Rabbit pops up beside Alice and says, slowly and aloud, why she is here (the kitten is lost across the board) and how to cross (watch the squares light up, then walk the same way). A tap, Space or Enter skips it; he then says it in one short line. The first squares light up only once he has finished speaking.
   - **The glowing path:** the way across lights up 4 squares at a time, one after another, each with a chime (do, mi, so, high do), then fades. Alice turns to look up the board and the camera rises while they show; she can't walk till they have. She then walks them from memory, each right square chiming its note and staying softly lit. Then the next 4 show, a little quicker each time. The path is 12 squares long, in 3 stretches. It winds: mostly up the board and along it, and now and then back down a row in a tight U-turn (about one board in four). It never crosses itself or runs alongside itself, and it reaches the far row only at its last square, next to the guards.
   - **A wrong square** wobbles like a seesaw, and Alice hops back to the start of that stretch, which shows again. There's no losing. Missed twice in one stretch, the Rabbit pops up with a tip ("count them: one, two, three, four!"), and once the squares have shown again, the square Alice should have stepped on sparkles three times as the kitten mews from it; missed three times, the squares stay lit. Squares she has already walked are always safe, and once the path is walked the whole board is.
   - **The chess pieces:** a White Knight, a Bishop and a Rook (ivory, with friendly faces) stand beside the path, low, middle and high up the board, never on it. They turn to watch Alice go by. The first time she comes next to one, it says a joke and a tip in the device's voice: the Knight always falls off his horse ("If a square wobbles, just try again"); the Bishop only walks corner-ways ("Your squares go up, along, and sometimes even back down"); the Rook isn't really a castle ("the squares light up four at a time"). A piece waits till the squares lighting up have shown. Walking into one: "Mind my toes!" (they can't be walked through). In 2D they are ♘ ♗ ♖.
   - **The guards and the lullaby:** three red pawn-soldier guards stand in a row in front of the kitten, just past the board's far edge, with crossed pikes at her sides. Alice sings to them from the far row. The lawn behind the guards is closed off, so the only way to the kitten is through the middle guard's place. Walking into a guard opens a music-box card on the right: three bells (gold, blue, pink) chime a tune, lighting up in turn, and the child taps them back in the same order. The first guard asleep needs 3 bells, the next 4, the last 5. A wrong bell just plays the tune again; "Play the tune again" replays it any time, and "Done for now" leaves him awake.
   - **Asleep:** a guard slumps, his pike drooping, with Zs floating up, for 30 seconds of play (the time doesn't run while a card is up), then wakes with a snort. Only while all three sleep do the pikes sink away from the kitten. Once all three are asleep at the same time, they sleep on (no more waking), and the middle one shuffles aside in his sleep: "Walk straight up to the kitten!"
   - **The kitten, then the maze:** stepping onto her square, she jumps into Alice's arms: "There you are, Kitty!" The kitten sticker 🐱 goes in the sticker book, with a banner, confetti and "A kitten sticker for your book!" A card takes them through the looking-glass into a small hedge maze. Dinah trots ahead and sits at each turn of the way, so the child can follow her. A side path is a dead end: a bump sends Alice back to the last turn she got right ("Oops, a dead end!"). There's no losing.
   - **The White Queen's castle:** through the maze's gate is a yard, with the White Queen in front of her castle. Dinah jumps into Alice's arms, and the Queen greets them in her own recorded voice (Jenny): "Ah, Alice! And you've brought Dinah's kitten…" Then a feast is laid in the yard: Alice stands at a long table between the Red Queen and the White Queen, who raise their glasses, "To Alice and Kitty!" The table shakes harder and harder and topples over, cake and all, as in the book. Everything spins and goes white… and Alice wakes up at home, sat on the picnic blanket by her sister, the kitten asleep in her lap. "Wake up, Alice dear!" "Oh, I've had such a curious dream!" The card says **What a curious dream! THE END.** **Play again** starts from the beginning. The game then counts as won, so the menu offers **Start adventure**, not Resume.

It plays as a **3D walk** through the hedges, with the camera just behind Alice so you watch her walk. For now it's always the 3D walk with this camera (the 2D map only without WebGL), and the 2D/3D and camera buttons are hidden.
- **Behind camera:** it sits a little to her right and well above her head, looking down along the path. Alice stays in view at the left, and the middle of the screen is clear for what's ahead.
- **Zoom:** the camera eases back and widens down long straight corridors. It comes in close when she stands beside someone, or while a riddle is up.
- **X-ray glow:** anything Alice can pick up (a tool, the DRINK ME potion, the Rabbit's gloves and fan, the Caterpillar's mushroom) glows gold through her whenever she hides it. Characters never glow.
- **Landing:** after the fall Alice drops into the Tea Party, lies dazed for a moment, then slowly gets up. The camera watches from in front of her, then swings round behind her, and only then can she walk.

On a keyboard, **C** still switches to over her shoulder 🧍 (close behind and to her right, at about her head height), then through her own eyes 👀, then back again; a mini-map overlay shows where she is (not in Follow the White Rabbit, nor once she's out of the Tea Party's maze).

The 3D walk is a real 3D scene ([Three.js](https://threejs.org) r159): leafy, lumpy hedges, growing in patches of slightly different greens, with woody branches inside showing through gaps in the leaves, flowers on their sides and tops, red-and-white toadstools at some roots, grass along their feet and fallen leaves on the gravel path under an evening sky, a low sun (the moon in Wonderland) casting shadows down the corridors and a light haze far off. The exit is a swirling portal in a ring of glowing stones (Wonderland) or a little cottage with lit windows and fireflies (Home). Alice is a 3D figure after John Tenniel's drawings (long fair hair and a black band, blue dress, white pinafore tied in a bow at the back) who walks, turns and casts her shadow. Creatures and tools are modelled in 3D after their paintings, lit and shadowed like everything else, and float a little above the path so they are easy to spot: wanderers trot, hop, march or scurry from tile to tile, the others turn to watch Alice, and each acts out being sent off (the Cheshire Cat’s grin lingers; the White Rabbit checks his watch and dives down a rabbit hole; the Mad Hatter dashes off and leaves his hat spinning; the Queen of Hearts twirls with the red paint and hurries off to paint her toes). The 2D map, mini-map and inventory keep the paintings. Without WebGL (or with `vendor/` missing) the game says so and plays on the 2D map.

## Settings (start menu)

The start menu shows only the chapter cards, the sticker book, **Sound effects**, **Music** and **Start adventure**. There are no other settings: the game is always The Story, on Easy (the only level), as a 3D walk (the 2D map only without WebGL) with waist-high hedges and the mini-map's usual fog. The old Map, View, Difficulty, Hedge height and Fog settings have been removed, and so has the "Easy · no danger" badge: in chapters with no hearts to lose, the top bar shows none.

Above them sit the **chapter cards**, one big card per chapter of The Story. A chapter reached once is remembered on that device and its card can be tapped to play it again from its start; the others show a 🔒. Below them is **My sticker book**, a slot for each chapter's sticker: a "?" until that chapter gives it (Chapter 1's stopwatch, the Tea Party's teapot from its unbirthday present, the Tulgey Wood's Cheshire Cat, and the Looking-Glass's kitten), then the sticker, kept on that device. **Start adventure** always begins at Chapter 1, and that chapter's card has a bold border with a soft ring round it, so it's clear which one will start; the other unlocked cards have a thin, quiet border. While playing, the chapter's number and name show in the middle of the top bar (just the name when the screen is narrow, like an iPad held upright).

- **Sound effects** and **Music** — separate switches. Sound effects include soft footsteps on the gravel and the garden's ambience: a breeze in the hedges, with birdsong at Home, wind-chimes in Wonderland, and an owl or a rustle in the leaves in the Tulgey Wood. The music is a gentle generated flute over soft strings (no files needed). With sound effects on, each chapter's opening is read aloud with the device's own voices: a soft, welcoming storyteller for the captions and the goal, and each character in a voice of their own (the White Rabbit a man's voice pitched up like a small animal). After the opening the game stays quiet, apart from a little door's one gentle warning and the Cheshire Cat's first "This way, my dear!"
- **Voices** — the storyteller, the White Rabbit and the White Queen speak in recorded voices (ElevenLabs: **Lily**, a soft British storybook reader, for the storyteller; **Gigi**, a friendly child-like voice, for the Rabbit; **Jenny**, for the White Queen). There is one MP3 per line in `voices/`, listed in `voices/index.json`; the game fetches them all as it loads, so they also play offline once it has been opened. Any line without a recording, and every other character, uses the device's own built-in voice (the browser's speech engine, so Apple's voices on an iPad). After changing a storyteller or Rabbit line, run `node tools/make-voices.js` to record it (`--list` shows the lines and sends nothing). It needs an ElevenLabs API key in `.env`, which is git-ignored; only lines with no recording are made, and recordings of removed lines are deleted. Each character's voice is picked once and kept all game. The White Rabbit speaks at the same easy pace in every chapter, and in his timed talks (the Tulgey Wood's opening, the bridge, the bush by the soldiers) each line waits for the last to be finished.

Fog mode, hedge height and fog transparency are folded away under the ▸ **Fog and hedge height** arrow. Settings are remembered between visits.

## Controls

Arrow keys or WASD — tap for one step, hold to keep walking (about 4½ steps a second), and keep holding (0.6 s) to run 30% faster.

- **3D walk:** ↑ walks forward, ↓ steps back, ← → turn 90°. Holding ↑ follows the corridor round bends where there's only one way on, and stops at junctions so you choose. **Tab** shows or hides the mini-map (top-right; it fills in as Alice explores). **C** switches the camera between just behind Alice (the default: it swings round her as she turns, and moves in when a hedge is close behind her; she turns see-through while she'd hide a creature, tool or the exit, and fades away when the camera is too close to fit her in, except while a riddle or puzzle card is open, when she stays solid) and through her own eyes, where the view bobs gently as she walks (not if your system is set to reduce motion). The camera choice is remembered.
- **2D map:** the arrows move Alice in that direction.
- **Walking by touch:** on an iPad or phone, touching and holding the scene walks Alice until the finger lifts (a quick tap is one step): in 3D straight ahead, round single bends (but on the chessboard, she turns towards the square touched and walks onto it), and in 2D towards the finger. It works in every chapter, and the arrow buttons stay too. A mouse doesn't walk her.

**C** 3D camera · **F** fog · **M** sound effects · **B** music · **R** start the chapter again · **Esc** menu.

- **Starting again:** ↻ (or **R**) first asks "Start this chapter again?" with two big buttons, **▶️ No, keep playing** and **🔁 Yes, start again**, so a stray tap never wipes the chapter. ↻ sits to the left of the sound buttons, away from ☰.
- **The menu pauses the game:** it sits above every card (so a riddle can't be answered through it), and while it's open the voices and music pause and the openings, the glowing squares and the feast stand still, picking up where they left off. On-screen arrows always float over the bottom-right corner of the maze, for a finger on an iPad or a mouse.

**iPad / iPhone:** open the game in Safari, then Share → **Add to Home Screen**. It then opens full screen like an app, with its own Alice icon (`manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`). This needs the game served from a web address the iPad can reach; a file opened straight from disk can't be added. Once opened, the game saves itself on the device (`sw.js`) and then plays without wifi; when online it picks up any new version. Every visit refreshes the one saved copy of the page (whatever its `?debug` or `?seed`), so the copy played offline is always the version last opened.

On Medium and Hard, running into any creature without its tool costs a heart and sends Alice back to the start; the tools she holds and the creatures already sent off stay as they are. On Easy a creature just blocks the way. Lose all hearts and you can retry the same maze.

## Handy URL options

- `index.html?seed=123` — the first maze uses this seed, so it's repeatable.
- `?debug&step=wood` — Start goes straight to the Tulgey Wood (`?debug&step=board`: the Looking-Glass; `?debug&step=castle`: the hedge maze and the castle).
- `?debug` — exposes `window.mazelandGame()`, `window.mazelandMusic()`, `window.mazelandView()` and `window.mazelandWorld()` (the Three.js renderer and scene) for browser-console testing (read-only use), plus `window.mazelandDraw()` to draw a frame on demand (handy when the browser slows animation in a background tab), and `window.mazelandPlayCroquet()`, which, once Alice is sitting at the tea table, skips the rest of the tea straight to croquet. The 3D canvas keeps its last frame, so screenshots can read it.

URL options need the page served over http (`.claude/launch.json` has a tiny Node server on port 8765); opening the file directly works for normal play.
