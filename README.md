# Alice in Mazeland

A local-only maze game. Double-click `index.html` to play in Edge or Chrome — no internet, no install. Keep the `vendor` folder next to it: it holds Three.js, which draws the 3D view.

Guide Alice through a hedge maze to **Home** 🏡 or **Wonderland** ✨. Creatures block the way; find the right tool hidden in the maze and walk into the creature to send it off.

**The Story** 📖 plays its parts in order, always from the beginning:

1. **Follow the White Rabbit** 🐰 is an intro, not a puzzle, and it can't be lost.
   - **The garden:** the left of the map is the lawn at home. Alice's house stands at the top, and her sister sits reading on a picnic blanket next to Dinah the kitten, who is asleep.
   - **The opening (about 12 s):** storybook captions tell the scene, and Alice asks "What is the use of a book without pictures?" Then the White Rabbit hops up the lawn: "Oh dear! I shall be late! Follow me, Alice!" Only one caption or speech bubble shows at a time. A tap, Space or Enter skips to his "Follow me"; Alice can walk once he has said it.
   - **The maze:** a small, low hedge maze on the right, with no fog and no mini-map, so the Rabbit always shows over the hedges and is the one to follow.
   - **The Rabbit:** he runs to each corner and waits there, tapping his watch, until Alice is close. Speech bubbles call her on ("Hurry, hurry!"), and paw prints show the way he went, fading after a while. On the way he drops his white kid gloves and his fan. Picking them up is optional, and he thanks her for them.
   - **Taps:** in the garden, tapping the sister, Dinah, the flowers, the house, the butterflies or the Rabbit makes them answer.
   - **The end:** he dives into a rabbit hole under a big old oak and drops a little DRINK ME bottle. Alice is too big for the hole until she drinks it and shrinks; then the hole pulls her in by itself, step by step.
   - **Map size:** the map is 25×17 on Easy, 27×17 on Medium and 29×19 on Hard. There are no creatures, tools or riddles.
2. **The fall**: a slow, dreamy nine-second fall down the rabbit hole, past cupboards, bookshelves, maps, jars and clocks (a tap or any key skips it). In the 2D view it's a "Down, down, down…" card. The White Rabbit loses his watch on the way down.
3. **Tea Party** ☕: the Queen's rose garden, with dark clipped hedges dotted with red and white roses, rosy gravel and golden evening light. Alice lands still tiny (30% of her height), too small for anyone to hear her: friends just block her way. In 3D the camera comes down with her while she's tiny, so the hedges tower overhead.
   - **The Caterpillar** sits on his red-spotted mushroom a short walk from the start, two turns in, before the first friend. Walking into him, smoke rings float up and a card asks "Who… are… YOU?" with three pictures: Alice, the White Rabbit and the Queen. Tapping anyone but Alice gets a puff of smoke and "I think not!" (try again). Tapping Alice earns a bite of his mushroom: she grows back to her proper size and he waves goodbye.
   - **The little door:** a tiny wooden door in a hedge near the start makes a shortcut to the Caterpillar, which only tiny Alice fits through. Once she's grown it's far too small. It never lets her past a friend.
   - **Paint the roses red** (optional): three white rose bushes stand in dead ends. Walking into one opens a card with its five white roses; each tap paints one red. The top bar counts the bushes painted (🌹 0/3). All three red earns a gold star ⭐ from the Queen, shown at the end. "Done for now" leaves the rest for later.
   - **The Cheshire Cat's grin:** when Alice seems lost (standing still for about 8 seconds, or three dead ends in a row), a floating grin fades in at the next junction on her way and points which way to go: to the Caterpillar while she's tiny, then to her chair at the tea party, then to the garden gate. He says something like "We're all mad here!", then fades away.

The Tea Party maze is 25% bigger than the others (29×17 on Easy, 43×23 on Medium and 57×31 on Hard). At its far corner it opens onto a lawn six tiles wide, on the right, where the tea party is laid.

There are no tools in the Tea Party and nobody can hurt Alice. The friends in the maze are drawn from the Mad Hatter, the Cheshire Cat, the Dog and the White Rabbit, and the Mad Hatter is always one of them (he hurries back to his seat at the table after). Walking into a friend brings up a riddle on their theme (the Hatter's are about hats, tea and cake, the White Rabbit's about time, the Cat's about grins and vanishing, the Dog's about bones and play), with three picture answers. The riddle card sits on the right of the screen, over the maze left undimmed, so Alice and the friend asking stay in view; the mini-map and the on-screen arrows step aside until it's answered (the Caterpillar's question and the tea party's puzzle cards work the same way). A wrong answer brings another riddle; three wrong answers send Alice back to the start, though friends she has already helped stay helped. A right answer makes the friend step aside.

**The tea party** ☕ is laid on the lawn past the maze. A ☕ marker shows it on the map and mini-map, even through the fog. Once Alice is out on the lawn the maze is done, so the mini-map goes away. Through the whole scene there's one thing to read at a time: each caption has gone before the next speech bubble, each bubble before the next line, and nothing is said in both.
- **The guests:** the Mad Hatter, the March Hare and the Queen of Hearts stand round a big table laid for tea. The Dormouse is asleep in a teapot on it. There's one empty chair, at the near end.
- **Arriving:** as Alice walks out onto the lawn, the guests cry "No room! No room!", then the Queen gives in: "…Oh, all right. Take a seat, Alice!" Walking into a guest gets a line from them.
- **Sitting down:** when Alice walks into the chair, the arrows stop working, the chair slides out and she sits. In 3D the camera rises behind her to watch the table.
- **Pour the tea:** "You pour, Alice, but mind whose cup is whose!" A card shows four cups and the four guests. The cups are hearts for the Queen, a top hat for the Hatter, a carrot for the March Hare and a moon for the Dormouse. Tap a cup, then whose it is (or the guest first). The right guest says thank you. Anyone else says "That's not my cup!" and hands it back, with no penalty. Once every cup is full, everyone sips.
- **The raven:** the Hatter asks "Why is a raven like a writing-desk?", Alice gives up, and he hasn't the slightest idea either. This part is just for fun.
- **Change places!** "I want a clean cup! Let's all move one place on!" A card shows the table from above, with each guest's cup in front of them and Alice's marked with a ⭐. The cups turn plain and slide one place round the table: once on Easy, twice on Medium, three times on Hard. Then tap the cup that's Alice's now. A wrong one shows whose it is, with as many tries as needed.
- **The Hatter's riddle:** "Let's see if you can solve MY riddle!" A riddle pops up with three picture answers. A wrong answer brings another riddle, as many times as it takes, and never sends Alice away.
- **The golden key** 🔑: the right answer earns it from the Queen. Alice holds it up, then gets up from the table and can walk again. The key shows in her hand and in the top bar.
- **Captions:** storybook captions tell each beat. A tap, Space or Enter skips to the next beat (not while a puzzle or the riddle is up). The puzzle cards have big buttons for a finger on an iPad, and work with a mouse, or Tab and Enter, too.
- **The exit** is a garden gate with a golden padlock, in the hedge at the lawn's far side. It stays locked until Alice has the key, then swings open, and a "Through the garden gate…" card (a tap or any key skips it) leads on into the Tulgey Wood.

4. **The Tulgey Wood** 🌲: an illusion maze, the Tea Party maze's size, in a dark, tangled wood: twisted trees above the undergrowth, glowing toadstools, fireflies and a purple mist. Alice is back at her proper size, and she's looking for the **Cheshire Cat**.
   - **The opening:** one line at a time: the chapter's title, a caption, Alice's "Curiouser and curiouser!", then the Cat's grin fades in to show the way: "Which way you ought to go depends on where you want to get to…" and "Come and find me, Alice… if you can!" A tap, Space or Enter skips it.
   - **Ghost paths:** two places on the way look like solid trees unless Alice faces them (up to 3 tiles straight ahead); turn away and the trees rise again. The mini-map shows them as trees until she has walked through.
   - **Fake paths:** four gaps off the way look open from the side, but the trees rise up when she faces them. Walking into one: "Only an illusion!"
   - **Shrinking and growing:** along the way, in order: a DRINK ME bottle 🧪, then giant tree roots only tiny Alice fits under; an EAT ME cake 🍰, then a thorn hedge only full-size Alice can step over; and the same again later. Each works as soon as she steps on it (an EAT ME cake waits if she's already full size). She can never get stuck: the tests walk 200 woods per difficulty.
   - **The Cat's riddle:** halfway, his grin hangs in front of a wall of trees and asks a riddle on the right-side card. As many tries as she needs (a wrong answer brings another). The right answer melts the trees away, showing a hidden path.
   - **Wood creatures:** a pencil-bird, a mirror-bird, an umbrella-bird and a horn-duck waddle about in dead ends off the way. Bumping one gets a scribble, a glint, a "Plip!" or a "HONK!"; they never block the way.
   - **The grin** still shows the way when Alice seems lost: to the riddle, then to the Cat, then to the portal.
   - **The end:** the Cat sits on a low branch of a big twisted tree, one step before the exit. Walking into him: "You found me! We're all mad here, you know." He fades away, grin last, and the portal glows open. Through it, "You found the Cheshire Cat!" ends The Story so far. **Play again** starts from the beginning; **R** restarts only the wood.

It plays as a **3D walk** through the hedges, with the camera just behind Alice so you watch her walk. For now it's always the 3D walk with this camera (the 2D map only without WebGL), and the 2D/3D and camera buttons are hidden.
- **Behind camera:** it sits a little to her right and well above her head, looking down along the path. Alice stays in view at the left, and the middle of the screen is clear for what's ahead.
- **Zoom:** the camera eases back and widens down long straight corridors. It comes in close when she stands beside someone, or while a riddle is up.
- **X-ray glow:** anything Alice can pick up (a tool, the DRINK ME potion, the Rabbit's gloves and fan, the Caterpillar's mushroom) glows gold through her whenever she hides it. Characters never glow.
- **Landing:** after the fall Alice drops into the Tea Party, lies dazed for a moment, then slowly gets up. The camera watches from in front of her, then swings round behind her, and only then can she walk.

On a keyboard, **C** still switches to over her shoulder 🧍 (close behind and to her right, at about her head height), then through her own eyes 👀, then back again; a mini-map overlay shows where she is (not in Follow the White Rabbit, nor once she's out of the Tea Party's maze).

The 3D walk is a real 3D scene ([Three.js](https://threejs.org) r159): leafy, lumpy hedges, growing in patches of slightly different greens, with woody branches inside showing through gaps in the leaves, flowers on their sides and tops, red-and-white toadstools at some roots, grass along their feet and fallen leaves on the gravel path under an evening sky, a low sun (the moon in Wonderland) casting shadows down the corridors and a light haze far off. The exit is a swirling portal in a ring of glowing stones (Wonderland) or a little cottage with lit windows and fireflies (Home). Alice is a 3D figure after John Tenniel's drawings (long fair hair and a black band, blue dress, white pinafore tied in a bow at the back) who walks, turns and casts her shadow. Creatures and tools are modelled in 3D after their paintings, lit and shadowed like everything else, and float a little above the path so they are easy to spot: wanderers trot, hop, march or scurry from tile to tile, the others turn to watch Alice, and each acts out being sent off (the Cheshire Cat’s grin lingers; the White Rabbit checks his watch and dives down a rabbit hole; the Mad Hatter dashes off and leaves his hat spinning; the Queen of Hearts twirls with the red paint and hurries off to paint her toes). The 2D map, mini-map and inventory keep the paintings. Without WebGL (or with `vendor/` missing) the game says so and plays on the 2D map.

## Settings (start menu)

For now the start menu shows only **Sound effects**, **Music** and **Start adventure**: the game is always The Story, on Easy, as a 3D walk with the usual fog and hedges. The other settings below are hidden and stay at their defaults.

- **Map** (a drop-down) — Home, Wonderland or The Story. Home and Wonderland change the colours, the exit and the ending; The Story is the three-part adventure above.
- **View** — hidden for now: the game always plays as the 🧭 3D walk.
- **Difficulty** — Easy (23×13, 3 creatures that stay put, can't lose) · Medium (35×19, 4 creatures, 2 wander, 3 hearts) · Hard (45×25, all 5, 3 wander, 3 hearts, thicker fog). Mazes are landscape and the board grows to fill a desktop/laptop window.
- **Hedge height** (3D) — Lowest · Very low · Low · Medium · Tall. Starts at Lowest; changing the difficulty leaves it as it is. Tall hedges hide everything beyond; Low ones come just above Alice's head, so from behind her you catch the tops of hedges further off; Very low ones reach her shoulders and the Lowest her waist, and you can see across the maze. Changes straight away, even mid-maze.
- **Fog mode** — only see what's near Alice (about 4 tiles on Easy, 3½ on Medium, 3 on Hard); explored paths stay dimly visible. The fog drifts slowly. In the 3D walk the fog covers only the mini-map; the scene itself stays clear.
- **Fog transparency** — 0–50% (default 2%): how much of the unexplored maze shows through the fog (in 3D, on the mini-map). Creatures and tools stay hidden until Alice has been near them.
- **Sound effects** and **Music** — separate switches. Sound effects include soft footsteps on the gravel and the garden's ambience: a breeze in the hedges, with birdsong at Home and wind-chimes in Wonderland. The music is a generated music-box waltz (no files needed).

Fog mode, hedge height and fog transparency are folded away under the ▸ **Fog and hedge height** arrow. Settings are remembered between visits.

## Controls

Arrow keys or WASD — tap for one step, hold to keep walking (about 4½ steps a second), and keep holding (0.6 s) to run 30% faster.

- **3D walk:** ↑ walks forward, ↓ steps back, ← → turn 90°. Holding ↑ follows the corridor round bends where there's only one way on, and stops at junctions so you choose. **Tab** shows or hides the mini-map (top-right; it fills in as Alice explores). **C** switches the camera between just behind Alice (the default: it swings round her as she turns, and moves in when a hedge is close behind her; she turns see-through while she'd hide a creature, tool or the exit, and fades away when the camera is too close to fit her in, except while a riddle or puzzle card is open, when she stays solid) and through her own eyes, where the view bobs gently as she walks (not if your system is set to reduce motion). The camera choice is remembered.
- **2D map:** the arrows move Alice in that direction.

**C** 3D camera · **F** fog · **M** sound effects · **B** music · **R** restart this maze · **Esc** menu. On-screen arrows always float over the bottom-right corner of the maze, for a finger on an iPad or a mouse.

**iPad / iPhone:** open the game in Safari, then Share → **Add to Home Screen**. It then opens full screen like an app, with its own Alice icon (`manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`). This needs the game served from a web address the iPad can reach; a file opened straight from disk can't be added. Once opened, the game saves itself on the device (`sw.js`) and then plays without wifi; when online it picks up any new version.

On Medium and Hard, running into any creature without its tool costs a heart and sends Alice back to the start; the tools she holds and the creatures already sent off stay as they are. On Easy a creature just blocks the way. Lose all hearts and you can retry the same maze.

## Tests

Needs Node 20+ (no packages to install):

```bash
node --test --experimental-test-coverage --test-coverage-include="tests/.extracted/**" tests/game.test.js
```

The tests pull the game-rules `<script id="logic">` out of `index.html` and check maze generation, fairness (every tool reachable before its creature, over 200 mazes per difficulty), movement, tools, wanderers, hearts, fog, turning, following corridors, ray casting and line of sight (what a hedge hides in 3D), the follow camera (never inside or against a hedge), the Tea Party (its clearing, the guests, sitting down, pouring the tea, changing places, the Hatter’s riddle and the golden key, the lawn and its gate, the Caterpillar’s question, the little door, the white roses, and the Cheshire Cat’s guiding grin), the Tulgey Wood (what lies along the way and in what order, the roots and thorns, DRINK ME and EAT ME, the ghost and fake paths, the Cat’s riddle and hidden path, the creatures, finding the Cat, and 200 woods per difficulty walked through), and full simulated playthroughs.

## Handy URL options

- `index.html?seed=123` — the first maze uses this seed, so it's repeatable.
- `?debug&step=wood` — Start goes straight to the Tulgey Wood.
- `?debug` — exposes `window.mazelandGame()`, `window.mazelandMusic()`, `window.mazelandView()` and `window.mazelandWorld()` (the Three.js renderer and scene) for browser-console testing (read-only use), plus `window.mazelandDraw()` to draw a frame on demand (handy when the browser slows animation in a background tab). The 3D canvas keeps its last frame, so screenshots can read it.

URL options need the page served over http (`.claude/launch.json` has a tiny Node server on port 8765); opening the file directly works for normal play.

## Third-party

`vendor/three.min.js` is Three.js r159 (MIT licence, `vendor/three.LICENSE`), the last release with a plain-script build, so the game still opens straight from the file. It logs one harmless notice in the console that this build style is deprecated.
